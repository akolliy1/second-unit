    Shots — slate and delivery

    This page shows two different things and keeps them apart on purpose.

    Studio slate ≠ delivery forecast

    • Studio slate — GET /api/shots, served from shots_catalog.py. Everything the production tracker has published: hundreds of shots, growing by 50 a day. It is what exists.
    • Delivery forecast — GET /api/fleet, Prometheus queries plus arithmetic in fleet.py. The handful of passes the farm is actually rendering, each with a measured rate and an ETA.

    A shot can be in the slate and never appear in the forecast — most of the slate is finished or waiting, and idle shots emit no telemetry worth querying. The two counts do not match and are not meant to.

    What state means in the slate

    • queued — published, render not started yet.
    • rendering — the tracker believes the pass is in progress today. This is the tracker's view, derived from ingest date plus expected render days — it is not a reading from the farm.
    • complete — the expected render window has passed.
    Slate state and forecast status are different vocabularies on purpose. "rendering" is a tracker expectation; "critical / at risk / on track / no completions in window" are measured from telemetry.

    Where each slate number comes from

    • frames — total_frames, the published frame count for the pass. Not frames remaining.
    • ingested — the day the shot was published to the tracker.
    • due — due_on, the client review this pass is booked against.
    • Filter counts — GET /api/shots/facets, slate-wide totals per value. They do not narrow as you filter, so the number beside "fx" is how many fx shots exist, not how many survive your other filters.

    Where each forecast number comes from

    • frames left — shot_frames_remaining, the raw gauge for that shot's pass.
    • rate — sum by (shot) (rate(render_frames_completed_total[15m])) * 60, i.e. completions per minute over the last 15 minutes.
    • forecast ETA — now + frames left ÷ rate. Straight division, computed in fleet.py. It assumes the current rate holds; that assumption is the forecast's only weakness and it is stated rather than hidden.
    • review deadline — shot_review_deadline_seconds{shot="SH042"}, read from telemetry. The production tracker publishes it; the agent discovers it.
    • slip — ETA − deadline, in hours. Positive is late.

    Reading the forecast status

    • critical — forecast more than an hour past review.
    • at risk — forecast past review at all.
    • on track — forecast lands before review.
    • pass complete — zero frames left. The pass finished: good news, not a fault, and drawn in a different colour from "on track" so the two are not confused.
    • no completions in window — frames remain but no frame finished in the last 15 minutes, so there is no rate to divide by and no forecast exists. Stalled, or the metric stopped arriving. Deliberately not green.

    Filters, paging and the URL

    Every filter, the sort, the page and the rows-per-page are written into the query string, so a filtered view is a link you can paste to someone else and it survives a reload. Paging is server-side: the browser never downloads the whole slate.

    New ingestion never moves the page

    The page polls GET /api/shots?per_page=1 once a minute and compares catalog_version. When it changes, a banner appears in the corner and nothing else happens — the table you are reading does not reload, re-sort or grow under your cursor. Refreshing is your decision.

    When the deadline cannot be read

    If shot_review_deadline_seconds is unreadable the sweep falls back to an invented time and sets deadline_is_placeholder. The page then leads with a red banner. An earlier version of the pipeline swallowed that failure and reported that a shot comfortably made a deadline which had already passed.

    Failure states are shown, not smoothed. A failed sweep renders an error with its reason and drops the rows; it never leaves the previous figures on screen where they would read as current. The same rule applies to the slate.
